📋 What each column in the table means
Stock Symbol
The short code for a company on the stock exchange. Companies are listed under a fixed abbreviation.
HDFCBANK = HDFC Bank Limited  |  RELIANCE = Reliance Industries  |  TCS = Tata Consultancy Services
P1 P2
Portfolio badge
Tells you which brokerage account the stock is in. P1 = your first account, P2 = your second account. Visible only in the "All Holdings" view.
Qty (Quantity)
How many shares you currently own of this stock.
If you have Qty = 274 for HDFCBANK, you own 274 shares of HDFC Bank. Click to edit if you buy or sell.
Avg Cost (Average Cost Price)
The average price you paid per share — calculated across all your buy trades for that stock. If you bought at different times at different prices, this blends them into one number.
Bought 100 shares at ₹500 and 100 more at ₹600 → Avg Cost = ₹550 (blended average of both purchases)
LTP (Last Traded Price)
The most recent price at which this stock was bought or sold on the exchange. This is the "live" current market price, updated every 60 seconds during market hours.
LTP = ₹1,833 means the last person who traded HDFCBANK on NSE paid ₹1,833 per share.
Day Chg (Day Change %)
How much the stock has moved today compared to yesterday's closing price. Shown in green (up) or red (down) below the LTP.
+1.45% = stock rose 1.45% from yesterday's close  |  -0.82% = stock fell 0.82%
Invested
Total money you put into this stock. Calculated as: Qty × Avg Cost.
274 shares × ₹1,406.27 avg cost = ₹3.85 L invested in HDFCBANK
Curr. Value (Current Value)
What your shares are worth right now at the current market price. Calculated as: Qty × LTP. This changes every 60 seconds.
274 shares × ₹1,833 LTP = ₹5.02 L current value. That's ₹1.17 L more than you invested.
P&L (Profit & Loss)
Your unrealised gain or loss in rupees on this holding. Green = profit, Red = loss. "Unrealised" means you haven't sold yet — it's a paper gain/loss.
Current Value ₹5.02 L − Invested ₹3.85 L = +₹1.17 L profit
P&L % (Profit & Loss Percentage)
Same as P&L but expressed as a percentage return on your investment. Easier to compare across different stocks.
₹1.17 L profit on ₹3.85 L invested = +30.4% return. This is more useful than the raw number when comparing stocks of different sizes.
🔔 Signal badges — RSI, Trend, and Calls
RSI (Relative Strength Index)
A number from 0 to 100 that measures how fast and how much a stock has been moving. It tells you whether a stock is being bought too aggressively (overbought) or sold off too hard (oversold).
0 — Oversold50 — Neutral100 — Overbought
Below 30Oversold — stock has fallen a lot, may bounce. A potential buying opportunity.
30 – 70Normal range — no extreme signal in either direction.
Above 70Overbought — stock has risen a lot fast, may cool off. Caution on buying more.
RSI = 24 → Stock has been beaten down heavily. Historically a recovery zone.  |  RSI = 78 → Stock has rallied hard. Avoid chasing it higher.
UP DN SW
Trend badge
Shows the overall direction the stock has been moving over the past several months, based on two moving averages (50-day and 200-day).
UPUptrend — price is above its 50-day AND 200-day average. The stock has been going up for months. Generally safer to buy in an uptrend.
DNDowntrend — price is below both averages. The stock has been falling. Buying into a downtrend is risky without a clear reversal.
SWSideways — no clear direction. The stock is moving up and down without a trend. Wait for a breakout in either direction.
BUY HOLD REDUCE

A suggested action based on combining all technical signals (RSI, MACD, Trend, Bollinger Bands). This is not financial advice — it's a technical snapshot to help you make an informed decision.
BUYMore signals point to upside. Could be a good entry or a chance to add more. Always verify the reason by clicking the stock.
HOLDSignals are mixed — no strong reason to buy more or sell right now. Stay put and watch.

📈 Technical indicators — what the charts show
MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)
Measures whether short-term momentum is stronger or weaker than long-term momentum. It compares a 12-day price average against a 26-day average.
BullishShort-term avg crossed above long-term avg → buyers are taking control.
BearishShort-term avg crossed below long-term avg → sellers are gaining the edge.
In the chart: green bars = bullish momentum building  |  red bars = bearish momentum building. The taller the bar, the stronger the signal.
Moving Averages (MA20, MA50, MA200)
The average closing price of the stock over the last 20, 50, or 200 days. They smooth out daily noise to show the overall direction. Longer averages (200-day) show the long-term trend; shorter ones (20-day) react faster.
MA20Short-term trend (4 weeks). Reacts quickly to price changes.
MA50Medium-term trend (2.5 months). A key support/resistance level watched by institutions.
MA200Long-term trend (10 months). If a stock falls below this, it's a serious warning sign.
If LTP > MA50 > MA200 → strong uptrend. If LTP < MA50 < MA200 → confirmed downtrend.
Bollinger Bands (BB Upper / BB Lower)
A price channel drawn around the stock's average price, ±2 standard deviations wide. About 95% of price action happens inside the bands. When price touches or exits the bands, it's statistically unusual.
Near BB LowerPrice is at the lower edge of normal — statistically cheap relative to recent history. Often bounces back.
Near BB UpperPrice is at the upper edge — stretched beyond normal. Often pulls back toward the middle.
Think of it like a rubber band. When it's stretched (near the bands), it tends to snap back toward the middle.
52-Week High / Low
The highest and lowest prices the stock traded at over the last 12 months. Important reference points — stocks often face resistance at their 52W high and find support near their 52W low.
52W High = ₹2,000, current LTP = ₹1,200 → "From High = −40%"
The stock is 40% below its yearly peak. Could be an opportunity or a warning — investigate why it fell.
From 52W High
How far the current price is from the stock's highest price over the last year. A large negative number means the stock has corrected significantly from its peak.
−50% or moreMajor correction. Could be deep value — or a falling knife. Research before buying.
−10% to −30%Normal healthy pullback. Often a good zone to consider adding.
0% to −5%Near the yearly high. Stock is showing strength, but less upside room before resistance.
🏢 Fundamental metrics — is the business actually good?
P/E Ratio (Price to Earnings)
How much you are paying for every ₹1 of the company's annual profit. The single most used valuation metric. Lower generally = cheaper, higher = more expensive (but may be justified by growth).
Below 15xPotentially cheap. Paying ₹15 for every ₹1 earned. Could be a bargain or a sign of problems.
15x – 30xReasonable for most established Indian companies.
Above 50xVery expensive. High expectations baked in — any disappointment can cause a sharp fall.
P/E = 20 means you pay ₹20 for every ₹1 the company earns. It would take 20 years to earn back your investment from profits alone (ignoring growth).
P/B Ratio (Price to Book)
Compares the stock price to the company's "book value" — the net value of all its assets minus liabilities. Useful for banks and asset-heavy companies.
Below 1xStock trades below its asset value — theoretically very cheap. But could signal hidden problems.
1x – 3xNormal range for most businesses.
Above 5xPaying a big premium over assets. Justified only if the business earns very high returns.
ROE (Return on Equity)
How much profit the company earns for every rupee of shareholder money. A measure of management quality and business efficiency. Higher is better.
Below 10%Poor returns. The company isn't generating enough profit from the capital invested in it.
10% – 15%Acceptable. Roughly matching the cost of capital.
Above 20%Excellent. The company is a high-quality compounder — these are the ones that make you rich long term.
ROE = 25% means for every ₹100 shareholders have put in, the company earns ₹25 profit per year. Compare: a bank FD earns ~7%.
EPS (Earnings Per Share, TTM)
The company's net profit divided by the total number of shares. Shows how much money the company earned per share over the last 12 months (TTM = Trailing Twelve Months).
EPS = ₹85 and LTP = ₹1,700 → P/E = 20x (which is: 1700 ÷ 85). Rising EPS over time is one of the best signs of a healthy business.
Revenue Growth
How much the company's total sales grew compared to the same period last year. Shows if the business is expanding.
+15% or moreStrong growth. Business is expanding rapidly.
+5% to +15%Steady growth. Consistent performer.
NegativeRevenue shrinking. Could be a red flag unless temporary (e.g. industry cycle).
Profit Margin
Out of every ₹100 in revenue, how much does the company keep as net profit? High margins mean the business has pricing power and low costs.
Revenue = ₹1,000 Cr, Net Profit = ₹150 Cr → Profit Margin = 15%. IT companies like TCS often have 20–25% margins. Banks and retailers typically have lower margins but high volumes.
Market Cap (Market Capitalisation)
The total value of all shares of the company combined. Tells you the size of the company. Calculated as: Share Price × Total Shares Outstanding.
Large CapAbove ₹20,000 Cr — stable, lower risk (e.g. HDFC Bank, Reliance)
Mid Cap₹5,000 – ₹20,000 Cr — growing, moderate risk
Small CapBelow ₹5,000 Cr — higher growth potential but higher risk
📅 Performance Returns (in the stock detail panel)
1M / 3M / 6M Returns
How much the stock price has changed over the last 1 month, 3 months, and 6 months. Useful for quickly spotting momentum direction without digging into charts.
+12% (1M) = price is 12% higher than it was one month ago — strong short-term momentum.
−18% (6M) = the stock has fallen 18% over 6 months — a downtrend worth investigating.
Your P&L vs. Stock Returns
Your personal P&L % (in the table) is based on your avg cost — when you bought it. The stock's 1M/3M/6M returns are based on the stock's price moves regardless of when you entered.
You bought SUNPHARMA at ₹686 (avg cost). Today it's ₹1,789. Your P&L is +160%. The stock's 6M return might only be +8% — because the stock has been at high levels. Both numbers are correct, just measuring different things.

💡 Quick tips for reading this dashboard
Don't act on a single signal
A BUY signal is strongest when multiple things agree: RSI is low, MACD is bullish, stock is in an uptrend, and it's near the BB lower band. One signal alone is weak. Four signals together is meaningful.
Technical vs. Fundamental
Technical signals (RSI, MACD, Trend) tell you when to act. Fundamental signals (P/E, ROE, Revenue Growth) tell you what to buy. Good investing uses both: pick quality companies (fundamentals) at the right time (technicals).
REDUCE ≠ Sell everything
A REDUCE signal means the risk/reward looks unfavourable right now. You might sell 30–50% of your position to lock in some profit and reduce exposure, keeping the rest in case the stock recovers.
Analysis refreshes every hour
The RSI, MACD, trend, and call signals are recomputed once every hour from fresh data. The time shown next to "Analysis" in the header tells you when it was last updated. Prices refresh every 60 seconds.
Click any stock for the full picture
Click any stock name or its signal badge to open the detail panel. You'll see the full 6-month price chart, RSI chart, MACD chart, detailed reasoning in plain English, fundamentals, and the latest news — all in one place.
This is not financial advice
The signals in this tracker are generated by mathematical formulas from public market data. They are tools to help you think, not instructions to follow blindly. Always do your own research and consider your own risk tolerance before buying or selling.
